The Origin of Dailymotion:

Dailymotion is a French video-sharing platform founded in Paris in 2005 by Benjamin Bejbaum and Olivier Poitrey. The idea came after a trip to New York: Benjamin Bejbaum wanted an easy way to share his holiday videos with other people. At the time, uploading and watching videos online was not as simple as it is today. The two founders therefore came up with a website where anyone could upload videos and share them with a wide audience. The project was initially called shortv.net. To get the company off the ground, the founders received support from private investors. In 2006, investment firms Partech International and Atlas Venture provided €7 million to help Dailymotion grow. Later, the French government and Orange also supported the company. The name “Dailymotion” combines the English words "daily", meaning “every day,” and "motion", which suggests movement or moving images.

The Timeline:

Dailymotion was founded in Paris in 2005 by Benjamin Bejbaum and Olivier Poitrey. After raising its first round of funding in 2006, the platform expanded internationally in 2008 and received support from the French government in 2009. Orange then invested in the company in 2011 and became its sole owner in 2013. In 2015, Vivendi acquired 80% of Dailymotion and shifted its focus towards audiovisual content and partnerships with media companies. In 2023, the platform launched a new app with a redesigned video-discovery experience. In 2024, it joined the Canal+ Group, strengthening its ties with the media industry. Finally, in 2026, Dailymotion launched DayOne, a programme that helps creators develop their projects through guidance and funding of up to €50,000.

New Features:

Dailymotion has launched a feed of short-form vertical videos that users can scroll through one by one, in a style similar to TikTok. Recommendations are based on the videos people watch and their preferences, helping them find content they might enjoy. This makes it easy to discover new videos without having to search for them. Creators can post short clips to showcase their work, share their ideas and reach a wider audience. The format is especially suited to mobile phones, where videos can be watched full-screen and users can easily move from one to the next. With this new feature, Dailymotion is modernising the way people browse content on its platform and hopes to attract more young users. The short-video feed also shows how platforms adopt popular formats to offer their audiences a familiar experience.
